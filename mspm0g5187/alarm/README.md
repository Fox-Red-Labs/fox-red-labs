# Alarm listener: the LP-MSPM0G5187 learns your smoke or CO alarm and tells you when it sounds

Put the LaunchPad development kit beside an ordinary smoke or CO alarm, press button S2, press the
alarm's test button, and from then on the board reports when that alarm sounds. A small neural network
on the chip's TinyEngine NPU listens through the onboard microphone for the two standard alarm patterns;
what it learned from your alarm (its tone and how loud it is up close) rejects the same pattern from a
TV, other beeping and everything else.

Status: Should work. Firmware, model and test tools are built and checked on the Mac. No board results are recorded; the [FAQ](../FAQ.md) lists the faults found.

Terms: [vocab](../../docs/vocab.md). NPU is the chip's neural processing unit; I2S is the audio bus; NNC is TI's neural network compiler. 1 kB = 1,024 bytes.

## What it listens for

| Alarm | Pattern (NFPA 72, each time plus or minus 10 percent) | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Smoke (temporal-3) | three 0.5 s beeps 0.5 s apart, then 1.5 s quiet, repeating | smoke |
| CO (temporal-4) | four 0.1 s pulses 0.1 s apart, then 5 s quiet, repeating | co |

## How it works

1. **Microphone.** ICS-40720 into the PCM1809 audio converter, I2S on PB10, PB11, PB12, 47.6k samples a
   second (TI's `device_uac_microphone_i2s` settings). The DMA interrupt filters and keeps every third
   sample: 15.9k a second (`fir_dec3.h`, 60 taps).
2. **Features.** Every 64.5 ms, two 512-point FFTs (CMSIS-DSP, q15) give 13 numbers: nine band levels,
   total level, the loudest tone's frequency, how tonal it is, and its level (`features.c`; the Python
   twin `host/features.py` is the specification, and the two agree to 0.0 dB median on test audio).
3. **Model.** Every 1.03 s the last 128 frames (8.26 s) go to a five-layer 1-D CNN on the NPU: 16 input
   channels (levels relative to the loudest frame, plus four channels locked onto the window's dominant
   tone), three answers: other, smoke, co. Trained on synthetic scenes (`host/synth.py`): real
   ringtones, alert sounds, speech and music from this Mac, and look-alike beeping (oven timers, backup
   beepers, microwaves, alarm clocks, alarm patterns with the timing 35 percent off, chirps, whistles).
4. **Decision.** An alarm needs the same answer in 6 of the last 8 inferences, and, once an alarm has been
   learned, the same pattern, a tone within 4 percent (at least 80 Hz) and a level no more than 10 dB below
   what was learned.
5. **Learn.** Press S2 (blue light blinks), then sound your alarm with its test button near the board.
   After four inferences that hear the pattern, the board stores the class, tone and level in data flash
   (0x41D00000) and prints `LEARNED`. Hold S2 five seconds to forget.

## Build and run

```bash
bash mspm0g5187/alarm/build.sh                                  # APP=capture: microphone to the Mac
APP=detect bash mspm0g5187/alarm/build.sh                       # the listener
<UniFlash>/dslite.sh --mode flash -c MSPM0G5187_<serial>.ccxml -e -f -v -u mspm0g5187/alarm/out/alarm_detect.out
python3 mspm0g5187/alarm/host/listen.py --ha  # log, Home Assistant, Mac notification
```

Before running: if the BP-CC1407P-9-FE radio is wired, take its orange (PB10) and purple (PB11) wires
off J20. Those pins are the microphone's clock lines. Put them back to restore the weather station
(`mspm0g5187/radio/README.md`).

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `alarm.c`, `detect.h`, `features.c`, `alarm.syscfg` | firmware (capture and detect from one source) |
| `fir_dec3.h`, `fe_tables.h` | generated by `host/make_fir.py` and `host/gen_tables.py` |
| `model/` | the NNC build of the model (`nnc_g5187_npu/`) and its held-out test vectors |
| `host/rec.py` | record the microphone to a WAV (capture image) |
| `host/bank.py`, `host/synth.py`, `host/train_alarm.py` | decoy sound bank, scene generator, training with TI's 8-bit QAT |
| `host/sweep_decide.py` | decision-rule sweep on held-out scenes |
| `host/check_features.py` | board features against Python on the same audio |
| `host/reel.py` | the live decoy reel the Mac plays at the board, and its scoring |
| `host/listen.py` | serial lines to a log, Home Assistant and a Mac notification |
