The kits our builds ran on

14 kits, from a 1 kB chip to Linux. Pick one on the rail; its page has what ran, the wiring and the fixes. A kit is here because something ran on it, not because TI sells it.

No radio: small chipsBluetooth, Zigbee and ThreadWi-FiLong rangeSpecial jobs: radar and motorsRuns Linux
  • LP-MSPM0C1104: Blink a light, read a button or a simple sensor. Too small for Zephyr. 24 MHz, 1 kB RAM. 4 builds ran on it.
  • LP-MSPM0L1306: Read light, temperature and small signals with the amplifiers on the chip; small programs, even a tiny AI model. 32 MHz, 4 kB RAM. 8 builds ran on it.
  • LP-MSPM0L2228: Show numbers on the small LCD glass on the board, like a meter, and keep a clock running from a super capacitor. 32 MHz, 32 kB RAM. 6 builds ran on it.
  • LP-MSPM0G3507: Small control and sensing projects, Zephyr, and small AI models run in software. 80 MHz, 32 kB RAM. 9 builds ran on it.
  • LP-MSPM0G3519: Bigger programs and more things plugged in at once. 80 MHz, 128 kB RAM. 12 builds ran on it.
  • LP-MSPM0G5187: Hear a sound or spot a pattern on the chip itself. 80 MHz, 32 kB RAM. 15 builds ran on it.
  • LP-EM-CC2340R5: Wireless sensors, buttons and lights, one radio at a time. 48 MHz, 36 kB RAM. 15 builds ran on it.
  • LP-EM-CC2755P10: Room for big wireless apps and the newer smart-home standards. 96 MHz, 162 kB RAM. 14 builds ran on it.
  • LP-EM-CC35X1: A smart-home device that joins Wi-Fi with no extra hub. 160 MHz, 1.1 MB RAM. 12 builds ran on it.
  • LP-CC1354P10-1: Sensors far from the house, past where 2.4 GHz reaches. 48 MHz, 296 kB RAM. 13 builds ran on it.
  • IWRL6432BOOST: Presence sensing on less power, with measured range. 160 MHz, 1 MB RAM. 1 builds ran on it.
  • LAUNCHXL-F2800137: Spin a motor or run a power supply, with sine and divide done in a few cycles. 120 MHz, 36 kB RAM. 7 builds ran on it.
  • TMDS62LEVM: A low-power Linux processor whose second core can run Zephyr as the real-time coprocessor, talking to Linux through shared memory. 0 MHz, 0 kB RAM. 3 builds ran on it.
  • SK-TDA4VM: A Linux computer with a deep-learning accelerator and video codecs, for camera and vision projects. 2000 MHz, 4.1943e+06 kB RAM. 4 builds ran on it.

RAM is working memory; flash holds your program. 1 kB = 1,024 bytes. Looking for a radio? The radio builds say what ran on each.
