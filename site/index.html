The build, step by step

  1. 1The LaunchPad and the panel, no cable in

    The MSPM0 G3519 on the bench with its USB socket at the top and no cable in, and the Waveshare 3.5 inch panel beside it, its 15-way connector toward the board's right-hand headers. Seen that way, J1 is the outer left column, J3 the inner left, J4 the inner right and J2 the outer right; pin 1 of J1 and pin 20 of J2 are the top pins. No power until the last wire is in.

  2. 2Power: two wires, 3.3 V only

    Panel VCC to J1 pin 1 (3V3) and panel GND to J2 pin 20 (GND). The panel's pin 2 (3V3) stays empty, and nothing on this build touches a 5 V pin.

    With the USB socket at the top: red on the top pin of the outer left column (J1 pin 1), black on the top pin of the outer right column (J2 pin 20). Nothing on the 5V pin beside 3V3.
  3. 3The screen bus: clock, data and chip select

    SCLK to J1 pin 7 (PB31), MOSI to J2 pin 15 (PB8), MISO to J2 pin 14 (PB7) and LCD_CS to J2 pin 13 (PB12). MISO is optional: the game never reads the screen back.

    Three wires in a row on J2 pins 13, 14 and 15 (two if you skip MISO), counted down from pin 20 at the top, and one on J1 pin 7.
  4. 4Screen control: data/command, backlight, reset

    LCD_DC to J4 pin 39 (PB9), LCD_BL to J4 pin 38 (PC4), LCD_RST to J4 pin 37 (PC5). J4 is the inner right column; pin 40 is its top pin.

    Three wires on the inner right column, pins 37, 38 and 39, the second to fourth pins from the top.
  5. 5Touch: four wires

    TP_SDA to J1 pin 10 (PC3), TP_SCL to J1 pin 9 (PC2), TP_INT to J2 pin 11 (PB14), TP_RST to J2 pin 12 (PB11). INT is optional: the game polls the touch chip. The panel's SD_CS pin stays empty.

    Thirteen wires in (eleven if you skipped MISO and INT). On the panel only pins 2 (3V3) and 7 (SD_CS) are empty. Tug each wire.
  6. 6Plug in USB, build and flash, then turn the panel face up

    Build with BOOT_GAME=1 BENCH=0 bash ports/touch-games/build.sh g3519 so it starts straight in 2048 (TI's Arm compiler; the script prints where touch-games-g3519.out lands). Flashing erases the demo that shipped on the board: back it up first (the board folder's AGENTS.md in the repository shows the DSLite memory reads that save the main flash). Then flash the .out through the board's own probe with DSLite, the flasher that comes with CCStudio, using a copy of the .ccxml file that carries your probe's serial. Turn the panel over so the glass faces you; the wires run underneath it.

    The panel's backlight comes on and it shows a 4 by 4 board with two tiles.
  7. 7Swipe to play

    Swipe in any direction and the tiles slide and merge. The build clocks the screen bus at 40 MHz, above the panel's rated clock of about 15 MHz: 2048 was seen playing on this panel at that clock, and a scripted swipe redraws in 32.6 ms. At 20 MHz the same code ran too, and a swipe takes 61.6 ms. MENU at the bottom of the screen goes back to the launcher, where Minesweeper is the other button: tap opens a cell, a long press flags it.

    Tiles move with the swipe and the score climbs. If a swipe does nothing, check the four touch wires first: J1 pins 9 and 10, J2 pins 11 and 12.
