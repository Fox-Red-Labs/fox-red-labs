Button, knob and dimmer on a breadboard

The board's S2 button toggles an LED, then a knob sets how bright it is.

EasyWiring neededOne sitting

You learn: Digital input with a pull-up, analog input, and PWM brightness.

Drag the bar to a step and let go to stop there; Play continues from it. Drag the picture to turn, scroll to zoom. Pin view looks straight down at the step. Tap a step, a part or a pin.
All 15 wiring steps
  1. 1Put the breadboard beside the LaunchPadSet the half-size breadboard on the right with its numbers running up the page. Letters a to j run across it and numbers 1 to 30 run along it, so every hole has a name like j7. The LaunchPad stays unplugged until the last step.The breadboard sits to the right of the LaunchPad. The LaunchPad has no USB cable in it yet.
  2. 2Ground first: LaunchPad GND to the blue railPush one end of a black jumper wire into the LaunchPad socket J2 pin 20 (GND) and the other end into the top blue rail. Wiring ground first means a part you plug in later is never live without a return path. A rail is a long strip of holes joined end to end, so any hole on it is ground.Both ends of the black wire show their tag: J2 pin 20 (GND) and the top blue rail.
  3. 33.3 V to the red railA red wire from J1 pin 1 (3V3) to the top red rail. Only the knob uses it in this build.
  4. 4Resistor in firstPut the 330 ohm resistor (orange, orange, brown) in holes h3 and h6. Without a resistor in series the LED would draw too much current from the pin. A series resistor is one wired in line with a part to limit the current through it.The resistor lies flat across three holes in row h, with its legs in h3 and h6.
  5. 5The LED, long leg toward the resistorPush the LED legs into f6 and f7. The long leg (plus, anode) goes in f6, the same column as the resistor's right leg. The short leg with the flat edge goes in f7. Backwards, it just stays dark.The long leg is in column 6, in line with the resistor's far leg. Nothing else shares column 7 yet.
  6. 6LED minus to groundA black wire from j7 (the short leg's column) to the ground rail closes the loop on the minus side.
  7. 7Signal wire from J3 pin 28A yellow wire from j3, the resistor's left column, to J3 pin 28 (PB27). The chip drives this pin to light the LED. A GPIO (general-purpose input or output) pin is one the program sets high or low.Yellow wire from breadboard j3 to J3 pin 28 (PB27).
  8. 8The button is already on the boardThe button is S2, the one on the LaunchPad's right edge, so there is nothing to wire for it. The program turns on its pull-up: a resistor inside the chip that holds the pin at 3.3 V until the button pulls it to ground, so the pin never floats.S2 is the button at the right edge of the LaunchPad, beside the headers.
  9. 9Run part 1Plug in USB and run the button program. Each press of S2 toggles the LED.LED changes state each time you press S2.
  10. 10The knob in the breadboardPress the 10 k knob into g23, g24 and g25. It has three pins: the outer two go across the supply and the middle one, the wiper, gives a voltage that follows the turn.The knob stands upright with its three pins side by side in row g.
  11. 11Knob to 3.3 V and groundA red wire from j23 to the red rail and a black wire from j25 to the blue rail put 3.3 V across the knob. 3.3 V, not 5 V: the chip's pins cannot take more than that.Red wire at the knob's left pin, black wire at its right pin.
  12. 12Wiper to J1 pin 2An orange wire from j24 (middle pin) to J1 pin 2 (PA14). That pin is an analog input: an ADC (analog-to-digital converter) turns the voltage on it into a number, 0 at 0 V up to 4095 at 3.3 V.Orange wire from j24 to J1 pin 2 (PA14). Turning the knob changes the number the program prints.
  13. 13Run part 2Run the knob program and watch the number in the serial terminal while you turn the knob from one end to the other.The printed number runs near 0 at one end and near 4095 at the other.
  14. 14Same LED, now dimmedNo new wire. The LED is on J3 pin 28 (PB27), a pin a timer can drive. PWM (pulse-width modulation) switches the pin on and off thousands of times a second; the longer it stays on in each cycle, the brighter the LED looks.
  15. 15Knob sets the brightnessRun the dim program. It reads the knob (part 2) and uses the number as the PWM duty (the share of each cycle the pin is on). Turn the knob and the LED goes from off to full.Knob to the left: LED off. Knob to the right: LED full. In between it dims smoothly.

What happens inside the chip

Button, knob and dimmer

Wiring. Knob wiper to J1 pin 2 (PA14), its ends to J1 pin 1 (3V3) and J2 pin 20 (GND). The LED is LED2 green on the board, J3 pin 28 (PB27); an outside LED through 330 Ω works there too. S2 is on the board, J4 pin 40 (PB3); optional: your own button on J2 pin 15 (PB8), other side to GND.

The steps

Steps

  1. Wire it on the breadboardBENCH

    With the USB cable unplugged, put the LED with its resistor and the knob on the breadboard and wire them as in the pin table. The button is S2 on the board, so it needs no wire.

    Printable wiring sheet

    You should see: Two small circuits on the breadboard, each ending at the LaunchPad. Nothing is lit.

  2. Build and flash the programTERMINAL

    Build and flash the program. Plug the board into your computer with the USB cable first.

    cd portability/hobby/button-knob-dim && ./build.sh --board mspm0g3519 --flash

    You should see: The build finishes and the flash reports done.

  3. Press S2BENCH

    Press S2, the button on the board.

    You should see: The LED toggles on each press.

  4. Turn the knobBENCH

    Turn the knob slowly from one end to the other.

    You should see: The LED gets brighter as you turn one way and dimmer the other way. The serial port (115200 baud) prints a line like adc=914 led=on duty=22 twice a second.

Read the serial output

Open the board's USB serial port in any terminal program at 115200 baud. The lines the program prints appear there.

The key lines

Each press of S2 flips led_on after a 20 ms debounce; the knob's reading becomes the PWM high time. the source

uint32_t now = hb_ms(); int btn = hb_in(HB_BTN_A) && hb_in(HB_BTN_B); /* low when either button is held */ if (btn != btn_prev && (uint32_t) (now - t_edge) >= DEBOUNCE_MS) { t_edge = now; btn_prev = btn; if (!btn) { /* pressed: the button pulls the pin to ground */ led_on = !led_on; } } uint32_t high_us = led_on ? ((uint32_t) adc * PWM_PERIOD_US) / 4095u : 0u; duty_pct = high_us * 100u / PWM_PERIOD_US; if (high_us != last_high) { last_high = high_us; hb_pwm(HB_LED, PWM_PERIOD_US, high_us);

If something goes wrong

The LED never lights laterTurn it around: the long leg goes to the resistor side.
The build stops and names a missing toolInstall that tool from the page it names and run the command again.
The flash cannot find the boardTry another USB cable: a charge-only cable looks the same.
Nothing happensCheck that the LED's resistor leg is on J3 pin 28 and its other leg on ground. The pins are listed in portability/hobby/button-knob-dim/PINS.md.
It only jumps between off and fullThe middle pin is on a digital pin or the outer pins are swapped; check the pin table.

How it works

S2 on the board pulls its pin to ground when you press it, and the chip's internal pull-up holds the pin high otherwise, so the program reads a low level for a press. It waits 20 ms after an edge before it believes the next one. The knob is a voltage divider: its middle pin sits between 0 V and 3.3 V, and the analog-to-digital converter turns that voltage into a number from 0 to 4095.

PWM switches the LED on and off 1,000 times a second. The share of each cycle that the LED is on sets how bright it looks, so the program turns the knob's number into that share. The program also prints the number, the LED state and the duty on the serial port twice a second.

Button, knob and dimmer

Expected result: S2 toggles the LED and the knob sets its brightness from off to full. The PWM rate is 1 kHz by design; the measured brightness range and build time: measured at the first run.

What this shows

  • A knob, an LED and one resistor connect to a LaunchPad with five wires; the button is already on the board.
  • Digital input, analog input and PWM output are three separate peripherals; this build uses each once.
What this does not establish, and limits

What this does not establish

  • That the program runs: nobody has run this build yet.
  • Any timing or brightness figure: those are measured at the first run.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The LED is driven straight from a pin through 220 Ohm.
  • The pins are listed in the build folder's PINS.md, one table per board.
