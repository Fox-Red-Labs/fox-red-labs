BeagleY-AI: errors and fixes

BeagleY-AI: errors and fixes

Getting startedModels on the C7xVideo decode and encodeThe USB webcam, the camera demo and powerContainers, programs and the operating systemFrigateWhen the board stops answering

Problems hit bringing up one BeagleY-AI 4 GB from a Mac on 2026-11-03. Versions: BeagleBoard Edge AI image 11.00.00.08 (built 2025-09-06), Linux 6.12.33, GCC 13.3.0, onnxruntime 1.15.0, GStreamer and FFmpeg as shipped on that image. 1 kB = 1,024 bytes.

Each answer starts with the fix.

    Getting started

    The Mac will not write to the card: "refusing disk4: not removable", or the write ends with an error

    Use the current beagley-ai/tools/write-card.sh: it accepts a card in the Mac's built-in SD slot, which lists as an internal, fixed disk, while still refusing the Mac's system disk and anything outside 16 to 512 GB. It unpacks the image to a file and pads it to whole 512-byte sectors before the raw write, because a raw write to /dev/rdiskN ending in a partial sector fails. It needs about 11 GB free in the work folder (third argument). The script's checks were run against the system disk and a 2 TB USB drive (both refused); a full card write with this version has not been run.

    No log was kept of the failed attempts; the script is beagley-ai/tools/write-card.sh.

    The board is powered from my computer's USB-C port but never shows up as a network adapter

    Reach the board over Ethernet. On the Edge AI image the USB-C port carries power only: there is no USB gadget, so no serial or network device appears on the Mac. Find the address in the router's client list (the board's host name is j722s-evm).

    Source: beagley-ai/FIRST-BOOT.log.

    Models on the C7x

    A model load never returns, and the next models hang too

    Reboot the board. Run one model per process with a timeout, as beagley-ai/bench/zoo/zoo_bench.py does with ONLY=<model folder> timeout 300 python3 zoo_bench.py 30, so a hung load costs one model and not the session. Seen once on 2026-11-03; no log of the hang was kept and the cause is not known.

    Pose takes 43 ms; the SK-TDA4VM's numbers suggested 25 to 30 ms

    Nothing is wrong; the 25 to 30 ms was our guess, scaled from the SK-TDA4VM's 13.76 ms and the 4 against 8 TOPS ratio. Both C7x cores report their 1.0 GHz clock (k3conf dump processor), and every one of the model's 274 nodes runs on the accelerator. TI's AM67A datasheet table gives 8.63 ms for YOLOX-nano and 4.94 ms for RegNetX; this board measured 10.15 and 5.95 ms with the Python timing used here, about 19 percent more, which is near TI's published speed but not equal to it; the pose model is not in that table, and about 36 ms is the figure TI's method would give (computed, not run). The SK-TDA4VM (8 TOPS) ran the same model in 13.76 ms.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/zoo/clocks-2026-11-04.log, beagley-ai/bench/zoo/zoo_tidl.log.

    Video decode and encode

    ffmpeg -c:v h264_v4l2m2m encode never finishes; dmesg prints wave5_vpu_firmware_command_queue_error_check: result not ready: 0x800

    Use GStreamer for hardware encode: v4l2h264enc ran at 110.1 fps on a raw 1088p file. FFmpeg's h264_v4l2m2m encoder did not finish in over 100 s, and on a second try waited in poll for over 3.5 minutes, ignored timeout's SIGTERM and wrote 48 bytes; kill -9 ends it and the codec still works afterwards.

    The cause is in the FFmpeg wrapper for the Wave5 driver. Source: beagley-ai/bench/video/ffmpeg-encode-hang-dmesg.log, beagley-ai/bench/video/wave5-pipe-fix-2026-11-03.log.

    FFmpeg hardware decode gives 3 frames and then stops when the output is scaled and piped

    Decode with GStreamer (v4l2h264dec) when the frames go to a program. ffmpeg -c:v h264_v4l2m2m -i clip.h264 -f null - decodes the whole clip (1.1 s for 300 frames at 1280x768), but with -vf scale=224:224 -pix_fmt rgb24 -f rawvideo - it delivered 441 kB (3 frames of 224x224x3) and then waited about 20 s with no error text.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/seam/run-2026-11-03.log.

    GStreamer v4l2h264dec ! videoconvert to a pipe runs at 6 fps, or seems to hang

    Scale the decoded frame before converting it: v4l2h264dec ! videoscale n-threads=4 ! video/x-raw,width=224,height=224 ! videoconvert ! video/x-raw,format=RGB. That ran 300 frames of the 1280x768 clip in 3.92 s (76.5 fps, 5 of 5 runs), and the seam app then ran at 61.3 fps with the classifier on the C7x. The other order, videoconvert on the full frame first, ran at 6.5 fps: the likely cause is that the CPU reads every pixel of the decoder's output buffers and those reads are slow (suspected; not measured on its own). At that rate 100 frames plus a model load overran the 90 s timeouts, which is what the earlier "failed" runs were. Leave capture-io-mode at its default and do not set n-threads on videoconvert after the decoder: both crash gst-launch-1.0 with Caught SIGSEGV, after which it waits for a debugger instead of exiting.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/video/wave5-pipe-fix-2026-11-03.log.

    dmesg prints wave5_vpu_firmware_command_queue_error_check: result not ready: 0x800

    Ignore it on its own: the driver prints it once at the end of every decode, including runs that finish with every frame.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/video/wave5-pipe-fix-2026-11-03.log.

    Decode and encode chained run at 35 fps though each alone runs above 100

    Give the encoder the decoder's buffers: v4l2h264dec ! v4l2h264enc output-io-mode=dmabuf-import. That transcoded 600 frames at 1920x1088 in 4.44 s (135.1 fps, 3 of 3 runs) against 17.09 s (35.1 fps) with the default, which copies every frame through the CPU.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/video/wave5-pipe-fix-2026-11-03.log.

    The USB webcam, the camera demo and power

    The webcam drops off USB: Non-zero status (-71) in video completion handler, then USB disconnect

    Power the board from a 5 V 3 A USB-C supply (BeagleBoard's requirement; it has no USB-PD and warns against unpowered hubs) and if the camera still drops, give it a powered USB hub (Insta360 lists the Link 2 Pro at 5 V 1 A); on a rated 5 V 3 A supply it held on the board's own hub in five 60 s starts. On a board fed from a hub port, the Insta360 Link 2 Pro dropped off at 1080p 7 of 7 times (MJPEG and H.264, with USB autosuspend off too) and after a gimbal move; 720p held 3 of 3 with the board idle but dropped once the board ran a Frigate container, and so did a microphone recording. It comes back about 5 s later with pan, tilt and zoom reset. Software settings did not change it; that it is the supply is the likely cause, not measured (no meter on the port).

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/camera-usb-drops-2026-11-03.log.

    Apps cannot find /dev/video-usb-cam0 after the webcam is plugged in

    TI's setup_cameras.sh makes that name only at boot. Install the udev rule from a Mac with beagley-ai/tools/camera/setup.sh <board address>: it names the first UVC camera /dev/video-usb-cam0 on every plug-in and sets its mains frequency to 60 Hz.

    Source: beagley-ai/tools/camera/99-usb-cam.rules.

    TI's Edge AI app runs at 1 fps with c7x_1: 0%; its log says SOC env var not specified. Defaulting target to arm

    Set SOC=j722s before starting app_edgeai.py. A login shell sets it from /etc/profile; a command run as ssh root@<board> "..." does not, and the app then runs the model on the Arm cores (737 to 809 ms a frame, 1.3 fps) and decodes in software. With SOC set, the people-count demo ran at 30.0 fps with the C7x 27 percent busy. beagley-ai/bench/camdetect/board_start.sh sets it. Also start the app with -n when it has no terminal (_curses.error: setupterm: could not find terminal).

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/camdetect/clip-run-2026-11-04.log.

    TI's detection demo crashes: IndexError: index 5 is out of bounds for axis 1 with size 1 in post_process.py

    Apply beagley-ai/bench/camdetect/people_count.patch: np.squeeze in PostProcessDetection also removes the box axis when a frame holds exactly one detection. The patch reshapes each output to one row per detection.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/camdetect/clip-run-2026-11-04.log.

    The board drops off Ethernet and reboots a few seconds after TI's camera demo starts

    Power the board from a rated 5 V 3 A USB-C supply and start the demo again.

    On a wall supply of unread rating, 4 of 5 starts ended with the board off the network and rebooting 5 to 15 s in (the fifth hung with an empty log; the first start streamed 29.9 fps for 5 s). On a rated 5 V 3 A supply, 5 of 5 starts held 60 s at 28.0 to 29.4 fps and the board stayed up. The cause is suspected to be the supply, not measured: the board has no console, no power meter and keeps no log. The boot log also prints tps65219 2-0030: Error IRQ trap undervoltage for LDO2 and BUCK2, on the rated supply as well, with no reboot after them. Source: beagley-ai/bench/camera-power/probe-2026-11-05.log, beagley-ai/bench/camera-power/e294-five-starts-2026-11-07.log, beagley-ai/bench/camera-power/supply-check-2026-11-07.log.

    How do I check that my USB-C supply is 5 V 3 A?

    Read the output line on the supply's label (for example 5V 3A, or 5V 3A among several USB-PD steps) and use a supply with a single output, since many dual-port chargers drop each port to 1.5 A when both are in use.

    The board has no USB-PD, so it needs a supply that offers 5 V at 3 A without negotiating. Nothing on the board reports the supply's rating; dmesg | grep tps65219 shows undervoltage lines, which do not name the supply behind them. For the 2026-11-07 runs the supply's rating is the bench owner's statement; the label was not photographed.

    TI's people-count demo shows a count of 0 with a person in the room

    Aim the camera at the person and check the stream's picture first.

    In the 2026-11-07 runs the Insta360 Link 2 Pro faced the bench, so the on-screen count was 0 while the detector ran at 28.0 to 29.4 fps; no person has been counted with this camera yet. Save a frame (beagley-ai/bench/camdetect/grab_stream.py) to see what the camera sees. Source: beagley-ai/bench/camera-power/camera-frame-e294-start1-2026-11-07.jpg.

    Do I need a powered USB hub for the webcam?

    Not on a rated 5 V 3 A supply at 720p: the Insta360 Link 2 Pro on the board's own USB hub did not drop in five 60 s starts of TI's camera demo, or in a 60 s run of Pop the bubbles.

    The earlier advice came from a board fed through a USB hub port, where the camera dropped at 1080p and under load (see the webcam entry above). Try the board's own hub first on a rated supply; add a powered hub if the camera still drops. Source: beagley-ai/bench/camera-power/e294-five-starts-2026-11-07.log, beagley-ai/bench/camera-power/p63-browser-60s-2026-11-07.log.

    The camera demo's start script prints STREAM FAILED

    Run pkill -f server.js on the board, then start again.

    A node server.js left from an earlier start keeps port 8081, and the start script's own pkill pattern (remote_streaming/server.js) does not match that command line. Source: beagley-ai/bench/camdetect/board_start.sh.

    Containers, programs and the operating system

    Pi-hole starts but DNS queries time out: failed to create listening socket for port 53: Address in use

    systemd-resolved holds 127.0.0.53:53. Start the container with FTLCONF_dns_listeningMode=BIND and dns.interface=eth0; dig @<board address> doubleclick.net then answered 0.0.0.0.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/docker/pihole-attempt1-LOCAL-mode-failed.log, beagley-ai/bench/docker/pihole.log.

    The board reads 80 to 87 degrees C during long runs

    The 4-pin fan connector was empty. Fit a fan for runs longer than a few minutes. No run was made with a fan and throttling was not checked, so any number here may be low.

    No temperature log was kept; the readings were taken by hand during the runs.

    cannot find -lc when linking with -static

    The image has no libc.a. Link dynamically; the stripped portable app is 66.1 kB.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/portability/three-ways-run.log.

    A program dies with Illegal instruction

    The Cortex-A53 has no ARMv8.1 atomics. Preload the trap library from am62l-evm/steam/lse_trap/ (built on this board with gcc -O2 -march=armv8-a -mno-outline-atomics -fPIC -shared -o lse_trap.so lse_trap.c -ldl; its two tests print PASS).

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/portability/lse-trap-run.log.

    /dev/kvm and /dev/fb0 do not exist

    The stock kernel is built without KVM (# CONFIG_KVM is not set), so a virtual machine needs a rebuilt kernel; Android's binder and binfmt_misc are off as well. /dev/fb0 appears only when a display is attached: the kernel has framebuffer emulation on top of DRM (CONFIG_DRM_FBDEV_EMULATION=y, tidss), and no display was attached in any run, so only /dev/dri/card0 exists.

    Source: beagley-ai/bench/portability/os-survey.log.

    Frigate

    Frigate's nginx exits with bind() to 0.0.0.0:5000 failed (98: Address already in use)

    Move your own service off port 5000: Frigate's nginx takes it when the container uses host networking. The detection server in beagley-ai/frigate/ listens on 5100.

    Source: beagley-ai/frigate/README.md.

    Frigate shows detection_fps 0.0 and the detector never gets a request

    Frigate's motion detector is still calibrating, and it only sends regions to the detector when it is not. It leaves calibration when a frame has under 5 percent changed area and 4 or fewer motion boxes, so a busy or moving scene never does. Set motion.threshold: 100 and contour_area: 600 for such a clip (a fixed camera does not need it).

    Source: beagley-ai/frigate/logs/tidl-c7x-clip.log, frigate/motion/improved_motion.py at v0.18.2.

    Frigate logs Error calling deepstack API: ... Read timed out

    The model takes longer than api_timeout (0.5 s in the sample config). The Arm-core model took 648 ms; set api_timeout: 3.0.

    Source: beagley-ai/frigate/logs/onnx-a53-clip.log.

    Frigate's camera input dies: Error during demuxing: No such device

    On 2026-11-03 the webcam dropped off USB (-71, then USB disconnect) within seconds of Frigate's ffmpeg opening it at 1280x720 MJPEG, in 2 of 2 tries, on the board's earlier power setup; with docker run --device the container's device file then stays stale. On 2026-11-07, on a rated 5 V 3 A supply, Frigate read the same webcam for 130 s at 59.71 ms per detection, so the supply is the likely cause (the same as the webcam entry above, not isolated here). Start the container with -v /dev:/dev so it sees the camera.

    Suspected: the power supply.

    Source: beagley-ai/frigate/logs/webcam-run.log, data/results/beagley-ai.csv (frigate-deepstack-yolox-nano-416-c7x-usb-webcam).

    When the board stops answering

    Checked 2026-11-08. Before flashing a risky image, run python3 tools/preflight_image.py <board id> <image.out>. Narrative and commands: docs/remote-board-recovery.md.

    How it locks up:

    • A model load on the C7x never returns and later model loads hang too; seen once (beagley-ai/FAQ.md, 2026-11-03)
    • ffmpeg h264_v4l2m2m encode does not finish and ignores SIGTERM; kill -9 ends it and the codec still works (beagley-ai/bench/video/ffmpeg-encode-hang-dmesg.log, beagley-ai/bench/video/wave5-pipe-fix-2026-11-03.log, beagley-ai/FAQ.md, 2026-11-03)
    • gst-launch-1.0 prints 'Caught SIGSEGV' and waits instead of exiting when v4l2h264dec has capture-io-mode set or the videoconvert after it has n-threads; a pipeline that converts before it scales runs at 6.5 fps and looks hung (beagley-ai/bench/video/wave5-pipe-fix-2026-11-03.log, beagley-ai/bench/seam/v4l2-after-reboot.log, beagley-ai/FAQ.md, 2026-11-03)
    • The USB webcam drops off USB (-71, then disconnect) at 1080p or on a gimbal move, and returns about 5 s later with its aim reset; 7 drops in 35 min (beagley-ai/bench/camera-usb-drops-2026-11-03.log, 2026-11-03)
    • Killing app_edgeai.py with pkill while it read an RTSP stream left it in uninterruptible sleep inside the Wave5 decoder release; dmesg showed 'wave5_vpu_firmware_command_queue_error_check: result not ready' and the next start failed with 'Error pulling tensor from GST Pipeline'; stopping with SIGINT and waiting 6 s did not hang (beagley-ai/bench/camera-lan/results-2026-11-08.log, 2026-11-08)

    Try in this order. Remote means the Mac does it with the board in place; hands means a person unplugs, presses or moves something.
