  • Does the SK-TDA4VM's SA2UL crypto block beat the Cortex-A72's crypto instructions for bulk AES and SHA on TI's Edge AI Linux?Works

    The TDA4VM's SA2UL crypto block is in the Edge AI kernel and the kernel prefers it (cbc-aes-sa2ul at priority 30,000), but no shipped tool reaches its AES from user space: openssl's afalg engine fails because CONFIG_CRYPTO_USER_API_SKCIPHER is off, and the cryptodev module the image carries has no openssl engine built for it. Driven through /dev/crypto by a 48-line program of this repo, the block does AES-128-CBC at 185 MB/s on 16 kB blocks for 16 percent of one core and 886 MB/s on 64 kB blocks with the core fully busy; the A72's own crypto instructions do 1,490 MB/s. SHA-256 over AF_ALG: 165 MB/s at 16 kB against 969 MB/s on the CPU. The hardware block loses on speed at every block size that could be compared and, through the ioctl path, saves no A72 time per byte for AES either.

  • Can the SK-TDA4VM's hardware video decoder feed the C7x with no Cortex-A72 copy of the pixels, and what does the zero-copy path save?Works

    Yes. With TI's zero-copy settings (v4l2h264dec capture-io-mode=5 feeding tiovxmemalloc), which by TI's description let the decoder write straight into buffers the C7x reads, a decode-to-detection pipeline (1280x768 H.264 into YOLOX-nano-lite 416 on the C7x) ran at 110.8 frames a second using 33 percent of one Cortex-A72. The same pipeline without those two settings, where the A72 copies every decoded frame, ran at 56.2 frames a second using 65 percent of a core. Zero copy used half the CPU time per second at twice the frame rate, about a quarter of the CPU time per frame; the copy was the largest extra cost measured, while the decoder alone does about 490 fps and the model 4.7 ms.

  • Does Windows on Arm run on the AM62L in a virtual machine?Could work

    Windows 11 22H2 (build 22621.1, arm64) installs as a KVM guest on the AM62L's two Cortex-A53 cores with 1.6 GB of guest RAM: firmware, boot manager, WinPE and an unattended Setup all run, and the installed system boots from its own disk twice. It stops in the out-of-box experience at 7,231 s with "Why did my PC restart?", which asks for a network to fetch an update.

  • What can the AM62L show on its HDMI screen besides Doom?Could work

    On the AM62L's own HDMI output, with no GPU, a looping showcase runs five things in turn: Doom straight to the framebuffer, TI's LVGL demo, Android 12 (Redroid) through scrcpy, SuperTuxKart and a RetroArch Game Boy core. 2D software paths are smooth (Doom 186 to 193 fps, RetroArch 48 fps at a 640x480 HDMI mode); anything that goes through software 3D or a video round trip is a slide show (SuperTuxKart 2 fps, Android about 1 frame a second on screen).

  • Does a model run on the LP-AM13E230 straight from the file the training tool wrote, in float, with no vendor quantization step?Works

    Seven models in the files their training tools wrote (float CNNs from torch, scikit-learn trees, XGBoost) ran on the AM13E230 through a plain Python-to-C step with matching answers; float as trained takes 0.8 to 1.2 times the int8 C build, the neural-network engine stays 2.4 to 8.9 times faster, and the XGBoost model is the most accurate and runs in 80 us.

  • Can the LP-EM-CC2340R5 drive a text display from Bluetooth messages?

    A Bluetooth text display firmware (Nordic UART to an SSD1306 OLED) builds in 195.1 kB of flash with 3.5 kB of RAM left, flashes and boots on the CC2340R5; the Bluetooth echo and the display are not yet run.

  • Can the AM62L run SNES and PlayStation emulation at full speed on HDMI?Works

    With no X server, no container and no OpenGL, a 300-line libretro frontend that draws straight onto the HDMI connector through DRM/KMS runs Snes9x 2005 at a locked 60 fps at every HDMI mode up to 1920x1080 (core 2.5 to 3.6 ms of the 16.7 ms frame, with audio and with a video thread), and PCSX-ReARMed at 60 fps on PlayStation test programs. The same Gambatte core that managed 48 fps and 10.4 fps in the showcase runs at 60 fps at 640x480 and at 1080p here. The display path was the problem, not the cores.

  • Can SimpleFOC, the open-source motor library, drive a motor from the LP-AM13E230 and the DRV8376EVM?

    SimpleFOC 2.4.0, which lists no TI chip, builds for the AM13E230 with none of its 3,097 compiled lines changed (23.1 kB of flash, 160 lines of port code plus a 146-line sketch) and its loop pass costs 1.35k cycles (6.74 us) open loop and 1.92k cycles (9.58 us) closed loop on the Halls with the motor supply off; the motor has not turned because the bus read 3.7 V

  • How fast is the LP-MSPM0G5187's NPU on MLPerf Tiny keyword spotting?Works

    The MLPerf Tiny keyword-spotting DS-CNN, quantized with TI's post-training tool and compiled by NNC for the TinyEngine NPU, runs in 18.70 ms (53.46 inferences a second) on the MSPM0 G5187 at 80 MHz with all 11 compute layers on the NPU and gets 91.0 percent of the 4,890 test clips right (89.7 percent on the first 1,000, under the closed-division bar of 90); the CPU build does not link and ResNet-8 does not fit the 32 kB of RAM.

  • Can Frigate use the BeagleY-AI's C7x accelerator as its object detector?Works

    Frigate 0.18.2 tracks two people and a dog with its detector on the C7x (64.46 ms per detection against 184.56 ms on its CPU detector); the USB webcam as the input drops off USB within seconds of Frigate opening it, 2 of 2 tries

  • Does the reference ROS driver for TI mmWave read the IWRL6432BOOST?Could work

    The reference ROS 1 driver for TI mmWave (radar-lab/ti_mmwave_rospkg) builds on ROS Noetic with one build-file edit and publishes every point of an IWRL6432BOOST walk recording, identical to the recording, when frames arrive every 50 ms; at the board's 250 ms frame period it publishes nothing, because its serial reader pushes a stale byte on each 100 ms read timeout and every frame then fails its length check

  • Does the popular Python mmWave SDK 3 reader work on the IWRL6432BOOST?Works

    The most-forked Python reader for mmWave SDK 3 point clouds (ibaiGorordo/AWR1843-Read-Data-Python-MMWAVE-SDK-3-) reads every point of two IWRL6432BOOST walk recordings, identical to the recordings, with its parser unedited; it needs NumPy 1.x (NumPy 2 stops it at the first point), and on macOS it reads nothing live until the port is wrapped so a read comes before the bytes-waiting count

  • Can the popular mmWave gesture recognizer run on the IWRL6432BOOST?Could work

    The most-starred open gesture recognizer for TI mmWave (vilari-mickopf/mmwave-gesture-recognition) reads none of an IWRL6432BOOST stream: its config loader stops on a low-power SDK cfg, and its frame parser, built for its own AWR1642 firmware's SDK 2 layout, rejects every frame on its length check; its models also want hand gestures within 1 m at 10 frames a second, so running it needs a format converter and new gesture recordings
