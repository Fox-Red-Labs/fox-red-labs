# Can an LP-MSPM0C1104 with 1 kB of RAM drive a 128 x 128 Sharp screen?

**Short answer.** Yes. Drawing one row band at a time, the chip showed a title, shapes and a live counter on the BOOSTXL-SHARP128 with 4.9 kB of its 16 kB of flash and 609 B of its 1 kB of RAM. A 2 kB frame buffer cannot exist on this chip, and none is needed. The cost is time: 154 ms for the whole picture with 1-row bands, 41 ms with 16-row bands.

Terms: [vocab](../vocab.md) (RAM, stack, SPI, linker). FAQ: [LP-MSPM0C1104](../../mspm0c1104/FAQ.md). Code and tests: [Sharp128 README](../../portability/boosterpacks/sharp128/README.md). 1 kB = 1,024 bytes.

**Question.** The panel needs 2 kB for a full picture and the chip has 1 kB. Does it fit at all, and what does it cost?

**Vendor position.** SLAU786 describes the panel and the pins and says nothing about RAM; TI ships no example for this BoosterPack on any MSPM0 kit (MSPM0 SDK 2.11.00.07, searched 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-20). The panel takes a picture one row at a time, each row addressed on its own (Sharp LS013B7DH03 specification), so a frame buffer is a choice and not a need.

**What was tried.** LP-MSPM0C1104 on probe MC110001 with the BOOSTXL-SHARP128 stacked, TI Arm Clang 5.1.1.LTS at -Os, SPI at 1.000 MHz read back from the chip. Expectation written before the run: flash under 8 kB, RAM under 700 B, the picture complete, and a full picture in 40 to 80 ms (wire time 21 to 24 ms plus drawing). The code is `portability/boosterpacks/sharp128/` (`sg_render_bands` draws the scene once per band; `examples/hello/hello.c`). The chip's SysTick does nothing, so the clock is TIMG14. Five band heights were built and run.

**How far it got.**

- Built: 4.9 kB of flash, 609 B of RAM with a 512 B stack.
- Ran: the program reached its counter loop (23 counts after 6 s at four a second) and stayed there; a camera frame shows the panel lit with shapes, too small to read the text; a look at the panel confirms the picture.
- Stack: the painted stack showed 352 of 512 B used; the generated linker file's 64 B default would overflow.
- Measured, one run each (whole picture drawn and sent): 1 row 153.7 ms and 609 B of RAM; 2 rows 92.7 ms and 625 B; 4 rows 63.3 ms and 657 B; 8 rows 49.3 ms and 721 B; 16 rows 41.1 ms and 849 B. One 1-row update takes 260 us. The bus carried a thousand bytes in 9.5 ms against 8 ms of clock.
- Stretch (video from a microSD card with Petit FatFs, no frame buffer): the player is built (7.2 kB of flash, 779 B of RAM) and runs on a Mac against a simulated card, 30 of 30 frames equal; on the board it stops at the card check because no card is on the bench.

**The line.** Not reached for the screen. The expectation held: the picture fits in 609 B and the time is inside 40 to 80 ms from 4-row bands up. The 1-row case is slower (154 ms) because the scene is drawn 128 times. The video path stops at **no microSD card on the bench** (error -2, card not ready); its frame rate is unknown. The simulated 30 frames a second counts bus time only.

- A card in the slot: copy `VIDEO.BIN` from `player/video_to_frames.py`, run `boards/c1104_run.py` on `player.out`, read `player_res` (frames, late, fps, card rate).
- Drawing faster: the scene redraw per band is most of the 154 ms; a scene built from rows or sprites would cost little per band.
- A faster byte loop: the bus runs at 84 percent of the clock rate because of CPU gaps between bytes.
- 256-byte reads with Petit FatFs (16 rows) to cut card reads from 16 to 8 a frame.

**Ask.** Has anyone driven this panel from a 1 kB part with a different method, or measured the microSD video path with a card? Reports are welcome.
