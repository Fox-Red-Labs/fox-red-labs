# What do you get from all the antennas of the TI IWRL6432BOOST radar, against using one transmitter and one receiver?

**Short answer.** 2x2 and 2x3 are measured; 1x1 is refused by TI's demo. With 2 transmit by 2 receive and with 2 by 3, an empty room for 60 s gave 0 flags in 240 frames, and a person sitting still at 2 m and at 4 m was held in 100 percent of frames. The person was first flagged 4 to 6 s after sitting down and the flag cleared 11 to 15 s after leaving, which is the demo's hold time. Walking in from behind the door was detected at about 6.5 m on 2x3. These are single passes with no spread, so a one- or two-second difference between 2x2 and 2x3 is inside the person's own timing. The tool applies an antenna setting over the radar's command line, runs voice-cued passes (walking in, sitting still, an empty room), and writes a customer-facing comparison page. TI's prebuilt demo does not read its command line while it runs with the shipped `lowPowerCfg 1`, so the first change after a boot needs one press of the board's reset button, and every setting is run with `lowPowerCfg 0`. Overnight in the empty test room, the 2x3 setting at a 12-frame hold gave 0 false alarms in 87,602 frames (6.08 h; 95 percent upper bound 0.49 an hour). TI's Wi-Fi presence model on the LP-EM-CC35X1, logging on the same clock for the first 1.19 h of that window, classed 0 of 2,065 results as present.

How to read the data and pick a presence radar with it: [the evaluation guide](../guides/evaluate-a-presence-radar.md).

Terms: [vocab](../vocab.md). The antenna setting is `channelCfg <receive mask> <transmit mask> 0`: 7 3 0 is three receivers and two transmitters (2x3), 3 3 0 is 2x2, 1 1 0 is 1x1. Board: [IWRL6432BOOST](../../iwrl6432boost/README.md). Page: [radar evaluation](../../site/radar-eval.html). Files: `iwrl6432boost/eval/`. Read and run 2026-10-10 on macOS 15.7 with Radar Toolbox 4.00.00.05.

**Question.** TI sells one 60 GHz presence radar family with 2 transmit and 3 receive channels. A product that only needs "is someone there" could use fewer antennas. What does the full set buy in distance, delay and false alarms?

**Vendor position.** The demo's source (`Motion_and_Presence_Detection/src/source/mmw_cli.c`) reads `channelCfg` as receive mask, transmit mask, miscellaneous control, counts the set bits as antennas, and errors if more than the maximum receivers are selected. In `motion_detect.c`: "Sensor Stop command is honoured only when Low Power Cfg is disabled". The data sheets for the IWRL6432 (SWRS298B), IWRL6432 WCSP (SWRS311), IWRL6432AOP (SWRS323B) and IWRL1432 (SWRS313A, 76 to 81 GHz) all list 3 receivers and 2 transmitters; the AOP part can use one transmitter at a time. Read 2026-10-10. No part in the family has a single antenna pair, so 1x1 is a setting of the same chip, not a cheaper part.

**What was tried.**

1. Read the demo source for what `channelCfg` and the rest of the presence configuration require. `compRangeBiasAndRxChanPhase` takes all 13 values whatever the setting, so it is left as shipped.
2. Wrote `radar_eval.py`: `accept` (apply each setting, read every CLI answer, check the stream), `empty` (empty-room false-alarm window), `run` (voice-cued walking and seated passes, each started on a timer or with Enter), `restore` (shipped configuration back). Presence is any zone flag in TLV 315; range comes from which box fired and the minor-motion range profile (TLV 303, 128 bins, 0.121 m a bin by arithmetic from the chirp settings: 256 real samples at 12.5 Msps with a 60.5 MHz/us slope, not calibrated against a measured distance).
3. Checked the analysis on synthetic frames: a seated pass with a known 2.5 s entry and 3.2 s exit gave 2.5 s and 3.25 s; a walk with known presence from 10 s gave 5.5 m at the 4 s a metre pace; an empty-room set with two known flags gave 2.
4. Tried the command line on the running demo: `sensorStop` at 125 attempts in 6 s, with and without carriage returns, got no answer and the stream carried on (consistent with the source comment above).
5. Read the stream at the shipped setting for 60 s: 231 frames, no missing frame numbers, 3.85 frames a second, 608 bytes a frame, TLVs 303, 306 and 315, four zones.

**How far it got.** Run, for 2x2 and 2x3. 1x1: configure and start refused. After one reset the demo took the 1x1 lines (`channelCfg 1 1 0`, everything else as shipped except `lowPowerCfg 0`) but `sensorStart` failed with `Error in setting up range profile:-40107`, and the command line then went silent (a second reset is needed). After a second reset 2x2 was accepted (25 of 25 lines Done, 32 frames in 8 s at 3.999 a second, 768 bytes a frame, TLVs 301, 303, 306, 315). The 2x3 setting started next in the same boot got every line Done but no frames, and the demo went silent again, so the demo seems to take one start per boot (suspected). The 2x2 and 2x3 passes are in the results below and in the findings log.

**2x2 results (2026-10-10, one boot, 60 s empty room, one pass each).** Empty room: 0 flags in 240 frames (95 percent upper bound 181 flags an hour, because the window is 60 s). Seated at 2 m: held in 100 percent of the 40 s, first flagged 5.6 s after the cue, cleared 10.7 s after the person left; range-profile peak at bin 22, which is 2.66 m by the arithmetic bin size, so the profile reads about 0.6 m long. Seated at 4 m: held 100 percent, first flagged 3.9 s after the cue, cleared 11.5 s after leaving, peak at 4.6 m, far zone flagged in every frame. Walking in: the person was already flagged at the 8 m start, so the arrival distance is at least 8 m and no more is known. The clear time of about 11 s is the demo's own hold time, not the person's walk. An earlier set on the same boot (listed in the results folder as early and backlog) is not used: the macOS serial driver delivered buffered frames late, and the person was still in view. If you see a 60 s window with far more frames than 4 a second times 60, drain the serial port to live frames before each pass; the tool now does.

**The line.** One receiver with the shipped range and angle lines is refused (-40107, range profile setup). Suspected, not tested: `sigProcChainCfg` (its azimuth FFT size of 64 assumes more than one receiver), `rangeSelCfg`, `cfarCfg` or `aoaFovCfg`, which a single antenna cannot satisfy; the fix is probably a 1-receiver variant of those lines, not run. The demo takes no command while it runs with `lowPowerCfg 1`, so the setting cannot change without a reset. What 1x1 would do to the stream is not known because the demo does not accept it with these lines. The 2x2 and 2x3 numbers are one pass per cell. Not run: whether the demo accepts one receiver with the shipped `aoaFovCfg` and `sigProcChainCfg` lines (angle estimation needs more than one virtual antenna; an error from the demo is itself the result).

- Use a point-cloud demo (TI's Out of Box or mmWave Demo images, in the toolbox) to get a distance for each detected object instead of box membership. That needs a flash, which costs two switch moves on this board.
- TI's toolbox ships a people-tracking configuration (`IWRL6432BOOST_Tracking_3Persons_7m_wall_mount.cfg`); the tracking image would give a track for each person, and needs a flash cycle.
- A corner-reflector pass for a fixed-range target.
- Measure power per setting with an external meter; the presence demo does not report it on this board.
- Repeat each pass three or more times and report the median and the spread.

**Ask.** If you have run a 1 transmit, 1 receive presence configuration on an IWRL6432 or IWRL1432, which settings did the demo need changed to accept it?

## Findings log

- The demo ignored the command line while it streamed. The cause is in the source: with `lowPowerCfg 1` the stop command is not honoured. So every eval configuration is sent with `lowPowerCfg 0`, and the first change after a boot needs one press of S2 (NRST).
- The 1x1 setting (`channelCfg 1 1 0`) was refused. `sensorStart` answered `Error in setting up range profile:-40107`, and the demo then stopped answering until the next reset. Suspected lines (not tested): `sigProcChainCfg`, `rangeSelCfg`, `cfarCfg`, `aoaFovCfg`.
- After the next reset, 2x2 started and streamed (4.0 frames a second, 768 bytes a frame). 2x3 started second in the same boot sent every line as Done but gave no frames and the demo went silent. Rule: one configuration start per boot; each further setting costs one S2 press. It is unknown whether 2x3 itself or the second start caused it; a later boot ran 2x3 first and it worked, which points at the second start.
- Spoken cues for the walking and seated passes have to make sense with the screen off: a cue that said only "enter" and "leave" left the person without a place to stand. Every cue is now a full sentence spoken twice (where to be, what to do, for how long, what happens next), and passes start on a 10 s timer.
- The first empty-room window read 1,746 frames in 60 s: the macOS serial driver had kept 12 minutes of stream while an answer was awaited, and clearing the port buffer did not clear it. That window and the first walk and seated passes (person still in view) are set aside (`-backlog`, `-early` in the results folder). The tool now reads and discards until frames arrive one at a time before each pass.
- Forwarding frames to the G3519 from the reading loop made a slow screen delay the radar reads (frames arrived in bursts of 14 with one timestamp). It now runs in its own thread and drops frames when full.
- In the 2x2 round the walking person stood at the 8 m mark in the room, in view of the radar. Presence was on at the first frame, so no arrival distance could be read (at least 8 m). For 2x3 the instruction became: start outside the room behind the door, open it on "go" and walk in.
- Second 2x2 set, with the Wi-Fi sensor running at the same time. Empty room 60 s: 0 flags in 240 frames. Seated at 2 m: first flagged 5.6 s after the cue, held 100 percent, cleared 10.7 s after leaving, range-profile peak 2.66 m. Seated at 4 m: first flagged 3.9 s, held 100 percent, cleared 11.5 s, peak 4.6 m, far zone on in every frame. The profile reads about 0.6 m long at both distances.
- 2x3 set (first start in its boot). Empty room 60 s: 0 flags after the first 14.2 s, which is the person leaving plus the demo's hold time (counted as carry-over, not a false alarm). Walk-in from behind the door: first flagged 6.7 s after the cue at about 6.5 m by the 4 s a metre pacing. Seated at 2 m: first flagged 6.2 s, held 100 percent, cleared 12.3 s, peak 2.78 m, far zone on in 20 percent of frames. Seated at 4 m: first flagged 4.3 s, held 100 percent, cleared 14.1 s, peak 4.6 m, far zone every frame. One pass per cell, so a difference of a second or two between 2x2 and 2x3 is inside the person's own timing.
- The two are not comparable. 2x2 has no usable walk-in (see the walking start position above), so the walk-in distance is measured for 2x3 only. 1x1 has nothing. One pass per cell; no spread.
- TI's CSI presence model on the LP-EM-CC35X1 ran alongside (2,075 results). Every result in the passes is class 1 (present), including the empty-room windows: 34 results in the 2x2 window (probability 0.91 to 0.99) and 34 in the 2x3 window (0.90 to 0.99). In this room the Wi-Fi presence model never reports clear in these passes, so it gives no clear time. The radar did: 0 flags in the empty windows. Same behavior as the earlier Wi-Fi presence experiment.
- After the 2x3 round the radar was left running the 2x3 configuration with `lowPowerCfg 0` (identical to the shipped configuration apart from that line) rather than sending the shipped file again, because a second start in the same boot is what hung it before. The stock `lowPowerCfg 1` file goes back at the next boot.
- A fast-clear configuration (`minor2emptyThre` cut from 20 to 6, `PresenceDetect-fastclear.cfg`) was tried to make the screen's presence badge clear sooner than the demo's hold of about 5 s. With a person seated at the bench the demo reported empty for 11 minutes 19 s and showed presence again only when the person walked in. The state machine re-detects a still person only on a larger motion once the window is short. Test a fast-clear setting on a seated person before trusting it. TI's page states the rule (a motion status is kept if at least one detection was recorded in the last `minor2emptyThre` frames) and does not say a short window stops holding a still person. Detail: [the thermostat page](2026-10-10-thermostat-mcu-only.md).
- The feeder that forwards radar frames and Wi-Fi results to the thermostat screen has to run detached from the shell; started as a background process of a command, it exits when the command's pipes close.
- The 12-frame hold: after one more S2 press, `PresenceDetect-hold12.cfg` (`minor2emptyThre` 12, about 3 s, `lowPowerCfg 0`, 2x3 antennas) was sent, with all lines Done in 7 s. It is the configuration of the overnight run.
- The overnight baseline ran in the empty test room (the rest of the house was occupied but quiet). The feeder logs every radar frame to a per-day frames file on the Mac and the Wi-Fi logger writes its results to a CSV. The window starts where the hold change took effect and the frame log resumed.
- The overnight result was computed by `iwrl6432boost/eval/overnight.py`: 87,602 frames at 4.00 a second (one gap of 20 frames) up to the first presence flag of the next morning, 0 presence frames, 0 frames with point-cloud points. **False alarms: 0 in 6.08 h**, 95 percent upper bound 0.49 an hour by the rule of three (row `radar_false_alarm presence-2x3-hold12-overnight`). Whether the morning flag was a person is not verified; the flags that followed (4.7 s, 9.5 s, then 121 s) look like someone passing through and settling. The walk-out clear time cannot be read from this log, because the person's exit at the start of the window was not timed. The Wi-Fi logger's loop stopped after 1.19 h (cause not found in its log); over those 1.19 hours it classed 0 of 2,065 results as present, median probability 0.013 (row `cc35x1 ai_presence_accuracy wifi-csi-empty-overnight`), against every empty-room window present in the evening passes with one person moving in the house.

**Cross-reference, Wi-Fi sensor at the same time (2026-10-10).** The 2x2 and 2x3 in-and-out passes were run with the CC35X1 Wi-Fi CSI presence demo logging on the same Mac clock. The Wi-Fi model said "present" in every window of every pass, empty room included, so "radar or Wi-Fi" equals Wi-Fi alone and "radar and Wi-Fi" equals the radar alone; "radar confirms Wi-Fi" kept the radar's near-zero misses but had about a third false alarms (113 of 337 moments in the 2x2 round, 112 of 338 in the 2x3 round) against the radar alone's 22 to 24%, mostly in the 11 to 15 s after the person left. Method, tables and limits: [the Wi-Fi presence page](2026-10-09-cc35x1-wifi-csi-presence.md), section "Side by side with the radar, 2026-10-10".
